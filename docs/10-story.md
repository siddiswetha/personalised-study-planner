# The story — Personalised Study Planner
![Bella — example student avatar](../assets/avatar-bella.svg)

**This is an EXAMPLE fictional story.** It is not a real student, a real quote, or a real result. It shows, in plain language, the problem this project solves.

## Meet Bella

Bella is a second-year student with four exams in one month. She is good at psychology and enjoys revising it, so she spends most of her time on psychology. Statistics is her weakest subject, and it carries the most marks, but she keeps putting it off because it feels hard.

Her generic timetable treats every subject the same. It does not know which exam is first, which subject carries the most marks, or which topic Bella finds hardest. By the last week, Bella has studied psychology twice over and has barely started statistics.

## Before the product

Bella works hard, but her time goes to the wrong places. She over-studies the comfortable topics and arrives unprepared in the high-weight, weak area. She feels busy, but not prepared.

## After the product

Bella enters her exam dates, the weightage of each subject, how hard she finds each topic, and the hours she really has each day, including her part-time job. The planner puts the high-weight, weak topics first and builds a day-by-day plan that fits her real hours.

Each day has a topic, a goal, a short recall task, and a break. When Bella is ill one day, she asks for a lighter day and the plan reschedules without losing coverage of the important topics.

## What the AI PM learns from this

- The problem is not lack of material. It is poor use of limited time.
- A plan that asks for more hours than the student has will be ignored, however good it looks.
- The student must be able to override any day. Control builds trust.
- Success is measured by coverage and preparedness before the exam, not by hours studied.
